FAQ

Common Questions

How do I report a civic issue? +

Open Report, add a description or voice note, attach a clear incident photo, confirm the Bengaluru location, and submit. Urban Pulse validates the evidence, analyzes the issue, and creates a trackable complaint.

What does the AI do with an uploaded photo? +

Florence-2 observes the visible scene, infrastructure damage, hazards, image quality, and uncertainty. Urban Pulse then combines that evidence with the complaint and location; uncertain or conflicting cases are marked for human review instead of forcing a confident answer.

How is a complaint routed in Bengaluru? +

The routing engine uses the issue category, location, ward evidence, severity, and configured authority directory to select a BBMP-aligned ward, department, operational unit, and escalation destination.

Does the complaint automatically reach a government authority? +

Urban Pulse prepares an authority ticket and supports configured email or webhook delivery, plus a manual official-portal handoff with reference tracking. Actual delivery depends on the authority channel configured by the deployment administrator.

Can nearby citizens verify an incident? +

Eligible nearby users can report that an issue is still present, worsening, resolved, or duplicated. The original reporter's identity and private evidence are not shown, and suspicious or conflicting activity is sent for review.

How can I track progress and confirm resolution? +

Open Complaints to view status, routing, authority handoff, timeline, and available evidence. When an issue is marked resolved, citizens can submit follow-up evidence so high-risk cases are not closed only from an unverified status change.

How are urgent incidents handled? +

High-risk evidence can trigger a safety gate, priority escalation, incident command record, response deadline, and alerts for opted-in users in the affected area. Automated signals remain reviewable by authorized administrators.

What could official government API access add in the future? +

With authorized government APIs, Urban Pulse could create tickets directly in official systems, receive acknowledgement and status updates, assign verified officers, synchronize resolution evidence, and give authorities a managed operational queue. This requires formal API access, secure government credentials, data-sharing agreements, and adoption by the relevant departments.

Complaint path

One record, from first report to closure.

01 Report

The issue, location, and available evidence are recorded.

02 Review

Visible conditions, risk, and missing information are examined.

03 Route

Ward and department rules identify the responsible team.

04 Update

Admins and nearby residents establish the current condition.

05 Close

Resolution evidence and the final status complete the record.

A deliberate boundary

The software supports judgement. It does not replace it.

Image observations, complaint text, location, weather, repeat incidents, and community evidence can reveal details that deserve attention. They are inputs to the case, not an automatic verdict.

Unclear images remain uncertain. Conflicting reports are marked for review. Priority, safety, routing, escalation, and closure remain traceable decisions that administrators can inspect.

01 / Accountability

The complaint does not disappear after submission.

Delivery attempts, status changes, escalation, and resolution evidence remain part of its history.

02 / Privacy

Public participation does not require public identity.

Community verification exposes the condition of an issue, not the personal details of its reporter.

03 / Continuity

External analysis is useful, but never a submission gate.

If an image cannot be interpreted, the complaint continues with uncertainty recorded for review.
